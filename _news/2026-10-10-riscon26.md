### Hosting the opening day

On the first day I hosted the main sessions:

- **Keynote** by Kamal Kishore, Special Representative of the UN Secretary-General for Disaster Risk Reduction and Head of UNDRR: _Cascading risks, emerging threats, and interdependencies_.
- **Panel discussion**: _Societal risk in an interconnected world: reflections on cascading risks, emerging threats, and societal interdependencies_.
- **Plenary**: _The economics of disaster risk reduction_, moderated by Margareta Wahlström, on how governments, financial institutions, insurers, the private sector and researchers can strengthen the financing of disaster risk reduction, with a focus on new financing instruments, evidence gaps and Swedish experience.

Over the two days I also hosted several of the parallel oral sessions. Meeting SRSG Kamal Kishore in person was a highlight of the week.

### A fully booked SESAC workshop on floods and accessibility

On 8 October, SESAC ran _Workshop E: Critical Infrastructures and Accessibility Analysis under Flood Conditions_ as part of the conference. Stefanos Georganos and I facilitated it, and Amalia Chantziara led the session. Registrations exceeded the 30 available places.

The workshop was an entry-level, hands-on introduction to Earth observation, GIS and spatial analysis, built around one question: **how does flooding change accessibility, and who is most affected?** In a guided Google Colab exercise based on a realistic Swedish flood scenario, participants combined flood hazard data, OpenStreetMap road networks, critical infrastructure locations and population data. The analysis showed how flooding can cut off access to hospitals and emergency services, even in areas that are not flooded themselves.

The feedback was encouraging. Of the participants who answered the evaluation, 80% found the level of difficulty right and 70% named accessibility analysis as one of the most useful parts. The discussions also opened up ideas for future collaboration between SESAC and CSR's RiskLab.

### Two co-authored presentations

**Analyzing hydrological and land cover changes driving water scarcity in indigenous communities in Southern Bahia, Brazil**, presented by Claudia Canedo Rosso, with Suanne Segovia-Tzompa, Marcia Fernanda de Camargo and me.
The study combines Landsat and Sentinel imagery with participatory mapping, workshops and interviews in indigenous communities. It shows how infrastructure growth, tourism, rising water demand, deforestation and expanding eucalyptus monoculture have reduced the quality and quantity of local water, and how linking satellite analysis with local knowledge can support community-led monitoring and more inclusive water management.

**Event-based windstorm exposure in Sweden using hazard and building footprints**, presented by Eleni Georgali, with Stefanos Georganos, Konstantinos Karagiorgos and me.
We used SMHI gust observations, MESAN (AROME) analysis data, Copernicus Earth observation predictors and Lantmäteriet data with XGBoost and neural networks to model footprints for 135 extreme windstorms between 2000 and 2024. Overlaying them with building data shows exposure concentrated in central and southern Sweden, and that storms with similar peak gusts can differ greatly in extent and severity, which is why event-based footprints matter for emergency planning.
