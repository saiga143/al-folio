Our study _Spatiotemporal Dynamics of Coastal Dunes along Southeast India: Using ALTM and UAV-Derived Elevation Data_ was presented at the 2026 annual meeting of IGCP Project 725, _Forecasting Coastal Change_, held in Brisbane, Australia, from 17 to 23 October 2026. Reshma K N of the National Centre for Coastal Research (NCCR), India, presented the work. I was not in Brisbane, but I co-authored the study with Reshma, Subbu Raj S, Preethi Sekar, Satya Kiran Raju Alluri, V Ramanathan and R S Kankara.

### Two questions, two coasts

Dunes store sand and shelter the land behind them. We asked how much the dunes changed between 2011 and 2024, and whether the way the shoreline moves explains that change. We looked at two sites on the Tamil Nadu coast with opposite trends: at Kanathur the shoreline retreats about 2.7 m a year, while at Marakanam it advances about 0.5 m a year.

### My part: from drone images to dune change

My work was the drone data processing and connecting it to dune change in response to shoreline change. The 2024 UAV surveys covered about 1.1 km of coast at Kanathur and 2.4 km at Marakanam: almost 2,300 images, flown with RTK-PPK geotags, processed with Structure-from-Motion into 3 cm digital surface models and orthomosaics. We compared these with a 2011 airborne laser (ALTM) map, outlined dunes on both with the same rules (raised ground, dune-like slopes, and a manual check on every outline), and linked the result to 36 years of satellite shorelines.

### What we found

- **Kanathur (retreating):** dune area fell by 57%, from 2.3 to 1.0 ha. The length of coast with dunes dropped by 35%, the dune line broke into twice as many pieces, and only 32% of the 2011 dunes survived.
- **Marakanam (advancing):** dune area fell by about 26%, but the length of coast with dunes grew by 18%, and 56% of the 2011 dunes survived.
- **The shoreline link:** at Kanathur, new dunes form only where erosion slows; at Marakanam, dunes survive best where the shore advances most.

Dune change has to be read in the light of whether the shoreline is retreating or advancing. For management, that means protecting the remaining continuous dune ridges, monitoring fragmentation, and prioritising high-resolution topographic surveys like these drone campaigns.
