### Hidden deficits: mapping urban deprivation across 5,000+ cities with Earth observation and GeoAI

The talk was based on our [Nature Cities paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44284-026-00498-w). More than a billion people live in slums and informal settlements, yet household surveys and censuses rarely show where deprivation clusters within a city. Urban form can fill that gap: building size and spacing, density and street access are visible from above, in every city.

<div class="row">
    <div class="col-sm mt-3 mt-md-0">

We classified neighbourhood-scale city segments in 5,132 cities across 103 countries in Africa, Asia and Latin America and the Caribbean. We estimate that about 395 million people (20.2% of the urban population covered) live in morphologically deprived segments, and that more than a third of them, around 136 million, live in small and medium-sized cities, not the megacities that usually get the attention.

The map is meant as a starting point for local investigation: a way to screen and prioritise areas alongside local assessment, not a substitute for it. The data are open on [Zenodo](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.17637264), the code is on [GitHub](https://github.com/saiga143/citysegmentdeprivation), and you can explore your own city in the [CSMD viewer](https://saiga143.github.io/csmd-viewer).
