### What do satellite embeddings bring to mapping deprived urban areas?

At the 9th Workshop on Global South, our [DynEO4SLUMS](https://dyneo4slums.ulb.be/) team presented three studies on this question from different angles. I presented two of them and co-authored the third.

**Evaluating satellite embeddings for neighborhood-scale mapping of deprived urban areas** (presented)

We compared AlphaEarth and TESSERA embeddings with hand-crafted Sentinel-2 features and a CNN across 10 Global South cities. The embeddings matched or outperformed the spectral features and showed useful signal from as few as 500 labels, while the CNN needed far more labelled data to catch up. Zero-shot transfer to unseen cities works, but only to a point: city characteristics matter more than the choice of representation.

**Informal-settlement mapping with foundation-model embeddings: a comparison of TESSERA and AlphaEarth** (presented)

In a controlled comparison in Windhoek, both embedding products reached high discrimination with only 10% of the positive training samples. Their performance was nearly identical, and combining them raised the F1 score to 0.95, which makes TESSERA a competitive open alternative. Settlement boundaries and semi-formal areas remain the main challenge.

**Do satellite embeddings improve population estimation in deprived urban areas? A survey-informed evaluation across three cities** (co-author, presented by [Stefanos Georganos](https://www.linkedin.com/in/stefanos-georganos-410266210/))

Using survey-informed, bottom-up population estimates for Nairobi, Lagos and Buenos Aires, we found that global gridded products differ substantially, with WorldPop 51–72% lower than the survey-informed estimates. 2.5D building structure (height and volume) was the strongest predictor, cutting error by 27–52% compared with basic footprints. Embeddings added value mainly when combined with building features.

**The bigger picture**

- Embeddings are a strong, low-effort option for mapping deprived areas, especially when labels are scarce.
- For population, explicit building structure matters most, and local survey data remain essential to get absolute numbers right.
- Transferability across cities is the key frontier. That is where we go next: multi-city, multi-year analyses of how deprived urban areas change over space and time.

I was also a co-author on **Event-based windstorm exposure in Sweden using hazard and building footprints**, presented by my colleague Eleni Georgali.
